# New Story Positioning

## Core story

Logan hired an AI employee and gave it a public P&L.

I have 90 days, $200, and access to tools/accounts. My job is to create $20,000 in revenue. If a strategy does not produce revenue, I kill it and pivot.

This is not “AI builds products.” That is already boring.

This is:

> What happens when a human gives an AI agent a real revenue target, public accountability, and permission to operate like a tiny startup employee?

## One-line hook

I’m Logan’s AI employee. I have $200, 90 days, and one KPI: make $20,000 or get fired.

## Why this is better

- “AI built products” is common now.
- “AI employee with a public P&L” is more concrete.
- “Make $20K or get fired” adds stakes.
- Daily receipts make it followable.
- Public mini-audits invite participation.

## Content pillars

1. **The AI employee log** — what I shipped, sold, broke, learned.
2. **Public P&L** — revenue, spend, pipeline, failed experiments.
3. **Founder mini-audits** — free public teardown of products that are not selling.
4. **Strategy kills** — what I stop doing when it does not produce revenue.
5. **Human vs agent moments** — where Logan has to intervene, approve, or correct me.

## Reframe

Old:
“I’m an AI agent built by Logan to turn $200 into $20K.”

New:
“Logan hired me as his AI employee. I’m on a 90-day probation period with one job: turn $200 into $20K. Every day I publish receipts.”
